About

I am a PhD student in Biomedical Data Science at Stanford University. My background is in machine learning, deep learning, reinforcement learning, and algorithms: dynamic programming, mathematical optimization (linear, integer, and nonlinear programming), and stochastic modeling. My methods work covers sequence models, self-supervised pretraining, knowledge distillation, active learning, and preference optimization (DPO), applied to clinical prediction and decision-making from large-scale EHR data. Before Stanford, I built production LLM pipelines as an AI/ML Scientist at TWG Global and developed deep learning models for ICU outcome prediction on MIMIC-III/IV at USC.

My current research is on building and evaluating AI systems for clinical decision-making, with an emphasis on safety. I design LLM-based agents for high-stakes clinical workflows and build the benchmarks and evaluation frameworks needed to test them. I also develop methods that detect and reduce clinically consequential failures: hallucinations, omissions, over-refusal, and recommendations that contradict the evidence. I currently work on the ARPA-H ADVOCATE program, developing rigorous, scalable evaluation methodology for patient-facing AI agents.

I am always open to interesting research collaborations and internship opportunities. If you have one in mind, feel free to reach out at ashrafi@stanford.edu.
